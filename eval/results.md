## What these numbers mean

**Honest refusal held at 1.0 across all three runs, with zero false positives on the thin band.** Keogh's Crisps, Barry's Tea and Ballymaloe Foods returned nothing, every time. Those three exist to catch a system that always finds something — including the name-collision trap where searching Barry's Tea surfaces an AI chatbot built for DPD. Nothing was invented about companies that publish nothing.

**Provenance violations were zero across 64 findings.** That is an invariant rather than a score: the gate cannot emit a finding without complete provenance, so anything above zero would be a bug in the gate.

**Recall misses its target at 0.556.** The cause is known and measured rather than guessed: domain-scoped queries were consuming the whole page budget with the company's own pages, so vendor-attestation sources — customer stories, the strongest evidence class in the plan — never got a slot. Sharing the budget across queries moved recall from 0.333 to 0.556 at no extra cost. Closing the rest is a page-budget question, and page budget costs money linearly.

**Over-claiming is the real remaining defect at 0.333.** One of three capability-present items was reported as deployed rather than undetermined. That is the Teamtailor case: the vendor ships opt-in AI, activation by that customer is published nowhere, and the correct output is a question rather than a finding. Guessing right for the wrong reason is precisely the failure this project exists to avoid, so this is the next fix, not a rounding error.

## What is not measured here

**Whether a quoted passage genuinely supports its claim.** Using a model to grade another model's output establishes that the two agree, not that either is right. The structural half is enforced — the quote must appear verbatim in the fetched page or the finding does not ship — but the semantic half needs hand-sampling and is not claimed as a number.

Run 1's recall of 0.000 was mostly a broken matcher comparing titles only, and the runner discarded the reports it scored, so it took a manual re-scan to tell a broken instrument from a broken system. Reports are now persisted to `eval/reports/` for exactly that reason. Run 1's `over_claim_rate` of 1.667 was likewise impossible — a rate above one — because it divided findings by companies. Of the faults these runs exposed, more were in the measurement than in the system, which is worth stating plainly rather than presenting the final numbers as if they arrived clean.

Role correctness fell from 0.958 to 0.793 between runs 2 and 3. That is not a regression: run 3 matched substantially more systems, including harder third-party ones, so the denominator grew and the easy cases no longer dominate.
